Privacy

Last updated: October 8, 2026

Tinker is a free, open-source AI assistant. This page explains, in plain language, what data it handles and where that data goes. The short version: we don't sell data, show ads, or keep your messages on our own server.

If you don't sign in

  • Your chats are stored only in your browser. Clearing your browser data deletes them.
  • Each message passes through the Tinker server to the AI provider to get an answer. The server does not save message contents.
  • For fair limits, the server keeps your IP address in memory for about a minute to count requests. It is not written to disk.

If you sign in

  • Sign-in is handled by Google Firebase Authentication (Google or email + password). Tinker receives your name, email and a user ID. Tinker never sees your password.
  • Your chats are saved in Google Firestore under your account, so they sync across devices. Security rules mean only you can read or change them.
  • In Settings you can export all your chats, or delete your account and every saved chat, at any time.

Things to know

  • Share links contain the whole chat inside the link. Anyone with the link can read it.
  • Your own Groq key (optional) is stored in your browser and sent through the Tinker server to Groq with each message. It is not saved on the server.
  • MCP access tokens you add are stored in your browser and sent to that MCP server when you chat.
  • The terminal app keeps chats and settings on your computer in ~/.tinker/. If you run tinker login, terminal chats are also saved to your account.
  • Tinker is not intended for children under 13.

Questions

Open an issue on GitHub. Tinker's code is public, so you can check exactly what it does.
